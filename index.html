Why predict first? Watching a trace feels like understanding, right up until the exam asks you to do it cold. Locating the important node yourself — successor, only child, or the leaf itself — is the part worth practicing.

Why deletion has three cases

Insert and search just walk down the tree. Delete has to rewire it, and the rewiring depends on how many children the node has.

No children

The leaf

Remove it and set its parent's link to null. Nothing else moves — you'll see it shrink away in the explorer.

One child

The bypass

The parent skips the deleted node and links straight to its only child. Watch the child slide up into the empty spot.

Two children — the tricky one

The successor swap

Copy the in-order successor's value up into this node, then delete the successor's old node down below.

Why the successor, specifically?

It's the smallest value in the right subtree — the next value up from the one you're deleting. It is larger than everything in the left subtree and smaller than everything left in the right, so the ordering still holds after the swap. (The predecessor — largest in the left subtree — works too. This site always uses the successor, so the traces stay consistent.)
